# Figure -> code -> data mapping

Every figure in the paper is regenerated by a script in `src/`, reads a data file in
`data/`, and writes a PNG to `figures/`. The per-figure numeric results are also saved as
CSVs so the exact values in the paper can be inspected without rerunning models.

## The four figures embedded in `paper/paper_v1.tex`

These four were regenerated under the canonical joint-stratified holdout (JSH) assembly and
are the only images the manuscript `\includegraphics`. Everything below this block is
supplementary.

> **Filenames do not match printed figure numbers.** The names were assigned when the paper
> had six figures; trimming to the 5-page limit removed the single-vs-pooled regimes chart
> and the TabPFN chart, so LaTeX renumbered the survivors. The printed number is whatever
> `\label` order gives, and the mapping is:
>
> | Printed in paper_v1.pdf | File on disk | Label |
> |---|---|---|
> | Fig. 1 | `fig1_coverage.png` | `fig:coverage` |
> | Fig. 2 | `fig3b_calib_dca_unseen.png` | `fig:calib` |
> | Fig. 3 | `fig4b_covid_unseen.png` | `fig:covid` |
> | Fig. 4 | `fig5b_fairness_unseen.png` | `fig:fairness` |
>
> Files were deliberately not renamed: the names appear in `src/09_paper_figures.py`,
> `src/09b_paper_figures_unseen.py` and the byte-identity regeneration checks. Cite figures
> by `\ref` in the manuscript and by filename in the docs; never write a literal figure
> number into the `.tex`.

> **Font sizes are set for the placed size, not the canvas.** `fig3b`, `fig4b` and `fig5b` are
> rendered on a 7.0-inch canvas but placed at `\columnwidth` (3.5 in), a 2x downscale, so their
> rcParams ladder is 16/14/12 pt in order to render at the intended 8/7/6 pt on the page. `fig1`
> renders 1:1 and uses a 9.5/9.0/8.2 pt ladder directly. If you change a canvas width, rescale the
> ladder by the same factor or the placed text will be illegible. Canvas HEIGHT does not affect
> on-page font size (only the width scale does), so height is the free parameter for page fit.

**Fig. 2's two calibration numbers are not in conflict.** The panel-a title says "Calibrated
*within* 0.046": "within X" is a worst-case bound, and 0.046 is the **maximum** absolute
decile gap (0.045994, decile 8) in `data/p1_calibration_unseen_deciles.csv`. The LaTeX caption
and the Results text say the **mean** absolute deviation is 0.015 (0.014718) over the same ten
deciles. A reader sees "typically 0.015, never worse than 0.046", which is exactly what the
manuscript body claims ("the mean absolute gap ... is 0.015 and never exceeds 0.046"). Both
values were re-derived from the CSV during the paper_v1 verification pass. No regeneration of
the PNG is required or appropriate.

Note that Fig. 3's unseen series comes from the documented within-unseen exception arm
(separate fit on 125,912 unseen-class 2015–2018 episodes, seed 42), while its trained series
comes from the year-restricted JSH refit. Fig. 3's two series are therefore *not* from the
same model, by design; see `docs/PROVENANCE.md`.

Notes:
- **Two rendering passes.** The `src/02b`--`08` scripts emit the `p1_*.png` figures in
  `figures/` on wide canvases, which is convenient for on-screen review. The manuscript
  embeds a *separately rendered* set under `paper/` (`fig1_coverage.png`, the three
  `*_unseen.png` panels; `fig2_regimes.png` and `fig6_tabpfn.png` are rendered but held as
  supplementary, their content stated in the text to meet the 5-page limit),
  produced by `src/09_paper_figures.py` and `src/09b_paper_figures_unseen.py`. These are not the same images: LaTeX rescales an
  8--12 inch canvas down into a 3.5 inch column, which shrinks all text by roughly 2--3x
  and prints tick labels well below the 9 pt body size. `09_paper_figures.py` rebuilds the
  same underlying results at the width they are actually printed at (3.5 in single column,
  400 dpi), so no rescaling occurs and the point sizes set in the script are the sizes that
  appear in the PDF. It reads only committed files under `data/` and reproduces every
  number it annotates (pooled JSH held-out AUROC 0.7913, Brier 0.1792: independently re-fitted
  during the v1 verification pass as 0.791438 / 0.179173; 8/18 maximum-training-size comparisons
  favouring TabPFN-3, mean -0.0001; matched mean +0.0183; 15/18 CIs including zero).
- The TabPFN comparison draws on **both** protocols: `data/p1_tabpfn_vs_gbt.csv` holds the matched
  10,000-row training size (its coverage rows are prefixed `COV:`), and
  `data/p1_tabpfn_uncapped.csv` holds the maximum-training-size protocol (TabPFN-3 at 100,000
  rows vs GBT at up to 1,000,000) together with the paired bootstrap CIs on the difference
  and a `delta_matched` column carrying the matched-training-size value for each
  substance-by-regime comparison.
- `07a` and `08a` are the compute steps that produce the intermediate
  `fairness_preds.parquet` and `p1_tabpfn_vs_gbt.csv`; both intermediates are committed so
  the plots can be regenerated without rerunning the heavy compute.
- **Unseen-class deployability (Figs. 3--5).** The manuscript's calibration, decision-curve,
  fairness and COVID-transportability figures are measured on the twelve *unseen* substance
  classes (n=215,983), not on the trained substances, since that is where the paper's coverage
  claim applies. `src/07b_unseen_deployability.py` fits the pooled big-6 GBT once and writes
  all four result CSVs from that single fit; `src/09b_paper_figures_unseen.py` renders the
  three figures from those CSVs alone (no refit). The trained-substance versions are retained
  as supplementary rows above and are the reference series in Fig. 3b.

## Bootstrap resample convention

All committed CIs use **300 resamples**, verified empirically during the paper_v1
verification pass by re-running each arm at 300/400/500 and comparing to the shipped bounds
(temporal-coverage arm: 12/12 rows match at 300, 0/12 at 500; COVID arm: 3/3 at 300, 0/3 at
400). `src/04`, `src/05` and `src/06` previously declared `BOOT_N = 500`, `500` and `400`
respectively and have been standardised on `BOOT_N = 300` to match the data they produced.
Point AUROCs are unaffected; only CI bounds. Test-set caps are per-arm and unchanged:
100,000 (`src/04`, `src/05`, `src/07_fairness_audit.py`), 150,000 (`src/06`).
